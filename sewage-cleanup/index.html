A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Bethlehem Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Bethlehem, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How Our Sewage Cleanup Works

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

1

Safety Assessment

First the crew identifies hazards, determines how much of the property the sewage has contaminated and puts containment in place to protect rooms that were not affected.

2

Extracting Sewage and Removing Materials

All standing sewage is extracted. Porous materials that were contaminated, such as drywall, insulation, carpet and padding, come out and go to proper disposal as biohazard waste.

3

Cleaning and Disinfecting

All remaining surfaces are thoroughly cleaned and disinfected using EPA-registered antimicrobial solutions. Airborne contaminants and pathogens are captured by HEPA air scrubbers.

4

Dry-Out and Deodorizing

We dry out any moisture that remains using commercial equipment. An industrial deodorizing treatment gets rid of the sewage smell. The space is confirmed safe to occupy by clearance testing.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Sewage Cleanup FAQ

Some things can be kept and others cannot. Glass, sealed tile, metal and other hard surfaces that do not absorb water are normally salvageable after disinfection. Anything porous the sewage touched must go, which covers mattresses, upholstered furniture, drywall, insulation, carpet and padding. For insurance purposes, our crew keeps a list of everything that had to be removed.
Removing the sewage and decontaminating the property is usually done in 1 to 3 days. If materials have to be replaced, full restoration can stretch to 1 to 3 weeks, based on how much was damaged. Our first priority is making the property safe to occupy again as fast as we can.
Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Grease buildup, wipes and other items that should not be flushed, and tree roots growing into the pipe are the usual causes. Sometimes an aging pipe cracks or caves in, or a storm overwhelms the city sewer and forces sewage up the floor drains. A camera run through the line after cleanup identifies the actual cause.

Expect roughly $1,500 to $3,000 when the backup is minor and contained, and more than $15,000 in cases of extensive flooding that need materials replaced and rebuilding done.

When to Call for Sewage Cleanup in Bethlehem

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
